"The rider paid $X. I got $Y. Why?"

A prototype of one new question for a driver earnings assistant. It answers from the trip's own breakdown, says plainly when a split looks bad, and never makes up a number.

Prototype on 40 synthetic trips. Not Uber data, not Uber's app or design.

Why it's built this way

The model writes words, not numbers

It refers to amounts as {placeholders}. The app fills them in from the trip's breakdown and rejects any number the model typed itself. A wrong figure here would destroy the trust it's meant to build.

It answers the question the driver actually asked

If the rider's quote includes a tip, or a rider discount the platform paid, it says so. If nothing on the trip explains the quote, it says it can't see that amount instead of inventing a reason.

It doesn't spin a bad split

On high-fee trips it states the platform's share and never calls it "normal." Where a line like insurance isn't itemized, it says so rather than estimating.

How well it works: 40 trips, three ways

Each answer is checked automatically: no invented numbers, the split stated, quotes explained, rider discounts explained, high fees stated honestly, missing data admitted, under 110 words. "All checks" means every check that applies to that trip passed.
